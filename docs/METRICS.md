# Pulse Metrics: Specification (algorithm version 3, shared formulas version 2)

This is the **single source of truth** for how Pulse computes its numbers. There are two implementations:

| Implementation | Path | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Python | `re-toolkit/metrics/` | Reference. Also runs on real band captures |
| Swift | `ios/PulseCore/Sources/PulseCore/Metrics/` | Runs in the app |

Both must give the **same results** on the shared test vectors in `shared/test-vectors/metrics_v2.json`
(generated by `re-toolkit/tools/make_metric_vectors.py`, checked by both test suites, tolerance 1e-9).

When a formula changes: bump the algorithm version, regenerate the vectors, and the app recomputes old days.

> **Honesty labels.** Each section says what comes from published research and what is **our own choice**.
> WHOOP's Recovery/Strain/Sleep formulas are proprietary. Ours are not copies; they're transparent alternatives.
> Pulse is for general wellness, not medical use.

## 0. Shared helpers (exact definitions matter for parity)

| Helper | Definition |
|---|---|
| `mean(x)` | left-to-right sum ÷ n (no compensated summation) |
| `median(x)` | sort; odd n → middle; even n → average of the two middle values |
| `sampleSD(x)` | √(Σ(x−mean)² ÷ (n−1)), needs n ≥ 2 |
| `lowerPercentile(x, p)` | `sorted(x)[floor(p × (n−1))]` |
| `Φ(x)` | standard normal CDF = 0.5 × (1 + erf(x / √2)) |

## 1. RR interval cleaning

**Why:** one missed or extra beat (common with optical sensors) creates a huge fake difference and wrecks HRV.

1. Reject every beat of **exactly 500 ms**. The band writes that value when it has no beat to report (about 5% of
   the beats on a real night). A true 500 ms beat (120 bpm) is lost too, which doesn't matter during sleep.
2. Reject RR outside **300–2000 ms** (200–30 bpm).
3. For each remaining beat, take the **median of the valid beats within ±5 positions** (11-beat window, clipped at
   the edges, including the beat itself). Reject the beat if it differs from that median by **more than 20%**.
4. Walking forward, reject a beat that differs by **more than 20%** from the clean beat right before it.

Each minute is cleaned on its own. Output keeps positions (rejected = null), so we know which beats are still adjacent.

*Source:* the 20% rule follows the classic Malik criterion used in the HRV standards (Task Force of the ESC/NASPE,
*Circulation* 1996; 93:1043–1065). Using a local median instead of the previous beat is a simplification of
Lipponen & Tarvainen, *J Med Eng Technol* 2019; 43(3):173–181.

## 2. HRV: RMSSD

`RMSSD = √(mean of (RRᵢ − RRᵢ₋₁)²)` over pairs where **both** beats survived cleaning and belong to the **same
minute** (a pair is never formed across two minutes). Needs ≥ 20 pairs.
*Source:* Task Force 1996 (RMSSD is the standard short-term vagal HRV measure).

**Overnight HRV:** walk through the sleep session minute by minute.
- Wherever **5 consecutive minutes** are all asleep (and worn), take their RR intervals as one segment, clean them,
  compute RMSSD, and jump ahead 5 minutes. Otherwise step 1 minute.
- Overnight HRV = **median** of the segment RMSSDs. Needs ≥ 3 segments.

*Source:* 5-minute windows are the Task Force standard short-term recording length. The median makes it robust to a
few noisy segments. *Our choice:* WHOOP reportedly weights the last slow-wave sleep. We can't detect sleep stages
reliably, so we use the whole night.

**SDNN (Apple Health only):** Apple Health stores HRV as SDNN, so the backup calculates it separately. Same walk
through the night; each 5-minute segment needs ≥ 120 clean beats, `SDNN = sample standard deviation` of those beats,
and the night's value is the **median** of the segments (≥ 3 segments). RMSSD stays the number used inside Pulse.
*Source:* Task Force 1996.

## 3. Resting heart rate

The **lowest mean HR over any 10 consecutive asleep minutes** in the main sleep (awake/off-wrist minutes break
the window).

*Source:* nocturnal minimum HR is a standard resting-HR proxy (Nanchen, *Heart* 2018; 104:1048–1049). The 10-minute
window is *our choice*, so that one quiet minute or an artifact can't fake a low value.

## 4. Sleep detection (HR + motion)

Per minute: `hr` = mean of valid per-second HR, `A` = **movement** (both null when not worn).

**Movement** removes the band's resting offset. On a real night the per-second activity sat at a steady 0 to 10 for
minutes at a time, changing only when the wrist settled into a new position, so the raw value says little about
moving. Sitting awake at a computer mostly reads 3 to 11, the same range. With `s` = the median of the minute's 60 values:
`A = mean(max(0, value − s))`, or the plain mean when `s > 12` (too high to be a wrist lying still).

1. **Cole–Kripke score** for each minute *i* (activity outside the data or null counts as 0):

   `D = (106·A₋₄ + 54·A₋₃ + 58·A₋₂ + 76·A₋₁ + 230·A₀ + 74·A₊₁ + 67·A₊₂) / 6000`

   *Source:* weights from Cole, Kripke et al., *Sleep* 1992; 15(5):461–469 (1-minute epochs).
   *Our choice:* the divisor (6000) is calibrated to **our** activity units, which aren't the 1992 actigraph's
   counts. It's tuned on simulated data now and **must be re-calibrated on real band data** (Phase 5).

2. **HR gate:** `T = lowerPercentile(worn minute HRs in the same block, 0.10) + 15 bpm`.
   A *block* is 24 hours starting at local **noon**, so each night sits in the middle of its own block and is
   compared with its own day. (A single threshold for a whole month fails on nights when your resting HR is a few
   beats higher than usual. Our tests caught exactly that.)
3. Minute is **asleep** if worn **and** `D < 1` **and** `hr < T`.
4. **Sessions:**
   - *Seeds* = runs of ≥ 10 consecutive asleep minutes.
   - Merge the next seed into the current session if the gap between them is **≤ 30 minutes**, or it is
     **≤ 120 minutes and at least 25% asleep**. A restless patch with several short wake-ups stays inside the night;
     a genuinely awake stretch doesn't. (25%, not 50%: Cole–Kripke also scores the 3–4 minutes *after* each
     movement as awake, so a restless patch that was mostly sleep only *measures* ~35% asleep. Found by the
     simulated-truth tests.)
   - Keep sessions spanning ≥ 20 minutes. A span under 180 minutes = **nap**.
   - Inside a session: `awake` = non-asleep minutes, `disturbances` = number of separate awake runs.
5. **Main sleep of a day** = the longest non-nap session that **ends** on that calendar day.

Steps 1–4 only find *candidates*. The app also joins two candidates up to **36 minutes** apart (a wake-up under 30
minutes plus the minutes around it that Cole–Kripke also scores as awake) when the second has at least 30 minutes
asleep. Sitting at a computer can pass all of this, so a candidate has to be confirmed (`SleepConfirmation.swift`,
Swift only):

6. **Onset:** a sleep starts at the first minute of **45 minutes** (90 if the band reports no motion at all) in which
   ≥ 85% of minutes have near-zero movement (`A ≤ 2.5`), no moving stretch is longer than 5 minutes, ≥ 85% of heart rates
   are within 8 bpm of the median, no heart-rate surge (2+ minutes, 10+ bpm above the median) happens with a still
   wrist, and the next 90 minutes hold at most one surge of any kind.
7. **Heart rate:** the sleep's median HR must be at least **5 bpm or 8%** (whichever is more) below the median of calm
   awake minutes in the 12 hours before it (after it, on the first night). Fewer than 25% of its minutes may be raised
   10+ bpm, with at most 2 surges per hour. With no awake data to compare, only a span of 3+ hours counts.
8. **End:** a wake-up under 30 minutes stays inside the sleep. **30 minutes up within 35** end it, where up means
   moving (`A > 2.5`) or a heart rate 8+ bpm above the sleep's median.
9. **Finished:** a sleep is shown only after **15 of the 45 minutes** after it are clearly awake (moving, or HR 10+ bpm
   above the sleep's), or the band was off the wrist for an hour, or 6 hours have passed without newer data.
10. **Minutes inside a sleep:** awake = no data, `A ≥ 6`, or HR 10+ bpm above the sleep's median. Restful = `A < 1` and HR at
    or below the median. Light = the rest. Asleep time, awake time, wake-ups and the phases all come from this one rule,
    so light + restful = asleep and asleep + awake = the length of the sleep.

The user's edits ("I wasn't asleep", new start / end) are applied last and survive every recalculation. A time the user
left unchanged follows the detection, so better detection can still correct it.

*Our choice:* all thresholds in steps 6–10. They are calibrated on **one** real night (2026-11-17/18, which includes the
awake evening before it) plus simulated nights, and **must be checked against more real nights**. Holding very still with
a low heart rate for 45+ minutes can still look like sleep, which is why a sleep can be edited or removed.

*Our choice:* the HR gate and the session rules. Actigraphy alone confuses "sitting still" with sleep; requiring
a low HR fixes most of that. **Sleep stages (REM/deep) are deliberately not estimated:** HR + motion can't
do it accurately enough to be honest.

## 5. Strain (0–21)

Banister TRIMP per minute, from mean minute HR:

`HRr = (HR − restingHR) / (maxHR − restingHR)`, clamped to ≤ 1. Minutes with **HRr < 0.2 are ignored**.
`TRIMP = Σ HRr × a × e^(b·HRr)` (Δt = 1 min), with **a = 0.64, b = 1.92** (male / unspecified) or
**a = 0.86, b = 1.67** (female).

`Strain = 21 × (1 − e^(−TRIMP / 130))`

*Sources:* TRIMP from Banister, in *Physiological Testing of Elite Athletes* (1991), and Morton, Fitz-Clarke &
Banister, *J Appl Physiol* 1990; 69(3):1171–1177. Max HR default `208 − 0.7 × age` (Tanaka, Monahan & Seals,
*JACC* 2001; 37(1):153–156).
*Our choices:* the 0.2 floor (so sitting around doesn't add up to "strain"), and the saturating 0–21 mapping with
k = 130. A hard 60-minute run (HRr 0.8) gives about 14.

Day strain uses all minutes of the calendar day. restingHR = that morning's resting HR, else the baseline mean, else 60.

## 6. Sleep need (minutes)

`need = goal (default 480) + 6 × max(0, yesterday's strain − 10) + 0.5 × max(0, yesterday's need − yesterday's sleep)`,
capped at 600.

*Source of the idea:* sleep debt accumulates (Van Dongen et al., *Sleep* 2003; 26(2):117–126).
*Our choice:* the coefficients. They're simple and explainable, not fitted.

## 7. Recovery (0–100%)

Needs tonight's HRV and resting HR, and **≥ 4 previous nights** in the baseline (the previous 14 days with data).
Otherwise there's no score; the app says it's still learning your normal.

```
zHRV   = (ln(HRV) − mean(ln baseline HRV)) / max(sampleSD(ln baseline HRV), 0.05)
zRHR   = (RHR − mean(baseline RHR)) / max(sampleSD(baseline RHR), 1.0)
ratio  = min(sleep / need, 1.1)
zSleep = clamp((ratio − 0.9) / 0.1, −3, 2)
c      = 0.6·zHRV − 0.25·zRHR + 0.15·zSleep
Recovery = clamp(100 × Φ(c), 1, 99)
```
Zones: green ≥ 67, yellow 34–66, red < 34.

*Sources:* comparing ln-RMSSD with a rolling personal baseline (Plews et al., *Sports Med* 2013; 43:773–781);
resting HR and HRV as complementary markers (Buchheit, *Front Physiol* 2014; 5:73); sleep and recovery
(Fullagar et al., *Sports Med* 2015; 45:161–186).
*Our choices:* the weights (0.6 / 0.25 / 0.15), the SD floors, and the Φ mapping. At your baseline it scores 50%,
and one SD better than normal scores about 84%.

## 8. Validation status

(Calories are §9, below.)

| Check | Status |
|---|---|
| Swift = Python on shared vectors | ✅ automated (both test suites) |
| Recovers simulated truth over 30 nights | ✅ sleep onset/wake ±10 min, resting HR ±1.5 bpm, HRV ±12%, recovery correlates r > 0.6 with the hidden readiness |
| Compared against a real device / polysomnography | ❌ needs the band (Phase 5+) |

### Known limitations
- **Asleep time reads low on restless nights.** Cole–Kripke's look-back scores the 3–4 minutes after each movement
  as awake, so expect roughly 3–4 minutes less sleep per wake-up. Re-check on real data before adding a correction.
- **The activity divisor (6000) is tuned on simulated motion.** Real WHOOP accelerometer units will differ. Phase 5
  must re-calibrate it with labelled real nights.
- **The first 4 days show no recovery score** (no baseline yet). That's on purpose: a number with no baseline would
  be made up.

## 9. Estimated daily calories (app only, never written to Apple Health)

Needs weight, height, birth year and sex. Without them, or without band data for the day, nothing is shown.

- **Resting (Mifflin–St Jeor):** `10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age`, then `+ 5` (male) or `− 161` (female), per day.
  Every minute of the day so far costs one 1440th of it.
- **Active (Keytel):** for a worn minute with an average heart rate of **≥ 90 bpm**, in kcal/min:
  male `(−55.0969 + 0.6309 × HR + 0.1988 × kg + 0.2017 × age) / 4.184`,
  female `(−20.4022 + 0.4472 × HR − 0.1263 × kg + 0.074 × age) / 4.184`.
  Only what it adds above the resting rate counts, and it never subtracts.

*Sources:* Mifflin et al., *Am J Clin Nutr* 1990; 51:241–247. Keytel et al., *J Sports Sci* 2005; 23:289–297.
*Our choice:* the 90 bpm threshold, because Keytel was derived from exercise and overstates low heart rates. It is an
estimate and has not been compared with a calorimeter.

### Bugs the truth tests caught while building v1
1. One HR threshold for the whole month split nights where resting HR ran a few beats high → per-noon-block threshold.
2. Five wake-ups within 45 minutes split a night in two, and the first piece became a fake "nap" → restless-patch merge rule.
